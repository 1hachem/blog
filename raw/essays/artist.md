# What it means to be an artist

We watch a percussionist play, hear their flawless tempo, see them lost in it, and we say: wow, what an artist.

We see someone on a skateboard kickflip off a curb, hands in their pockets, chewing gum, headphones on: wow, what a cool guy.

We watch a good actor and forget they're acting. We're so immersed that at some point we think it must be easy. If you've ever tried to act in front of a camera, you know it isn't.

So what do the people we call artists have in common? They make it look effortless.

Being a master of anything comes down to two things: knowing **when** to act, and knowing the **minimum** it takes, then applying only that. On a skateboard, too much force throws you off, and the right force at the wrong moment throws you off too. Stiff or flailing, either way you look clumsy.

Timing matters because nothing stays still. Everything comes in pairs: up and down, male and female, yin and yang. And pairs alternate. A bird beats its wings down, then draws them back up. You inhale, then exhale. You wake, then sleep; eat, then excrete. Alternation is a cycle, and the world runs on cycles. Planets spin on their axes and circle the sun, and moons circle the planets. We go from atoms in the ground to standing humans and back to the earth.

Know the cycles of a thing and you can make it flourish. Raising a child is knowing when they eat, when they sleep, and when they wake. A good speaker is also a good listener: they know when to speak, how to say it, and when to stay silent. A good teacher knows when to explain and when to let the kids play, when to challenge and when to praise. A startup is a system of cycles too. Live in meetings and no work gets done. Skip them and everyone drifts out of alignment, and the effort is wasted. Overload people and they burn out. Underload them and they go looking for work elsewhere.

Knowing the cycles lets you act correctly, and therefore effortlessly. You stop fighting how things work and move in time with them.

This is what islam means in its literal sense: surrender. But surrendering to the order of things doesn't make you a leaf in the wind. It's the opposite. No sailor can fight the wind, yet a sailboat can travel against it. It tacks: it angles its sail so the wind drives it forward on a diagonal, then turns and does the same on the other side, zigzagging upwind with the very force it is heading into.

It works because a sail isn't a wall the wind pushes on. It's a wing. Air flowing across its curve pulls the sail sideways and slightly forward, the way air lifts a plane. Under the boat, the keel, a deep fin, refuses to slide sideways, so the only way left to move is forward. No boat can point straight into the wind, but at about 45° to it, it can sail. Turn across the wind, sail 45° on the other side, and keep alternating. Each leg goes a bit further upwind. Tacking, too, is a cycle.

To surrender is to accept the fundamental truths, stop resisting them, and put them to work for you. Stop fighting reality in your startup and in your marriage. Study how it actually works, and change your approach until it lines up with it.

That is what the percussionist knows. Tempo is just a cycle you've learned to live inside.
